# Blog Content Writing Guide

> **Status: Task-derived draft — pending editorial approval.**
> Source: MM03-01 task requirements, recorded decisions and active task notes. This is not a replacement for the existing Mobelaris writing guide. Unspecified house rules remain open.

## Scope

- Blog posts only.
- New English (UK) content only.
- Write for the supplied primary and related keywords; do not conduct or revise keyword research.
- Treat optional client insights as useful first-hand context when present, never as verified factual evidence.

## Required writing approach

- Use answer-first writing.
- Put the bottom line up front in key takeaways and the conclusion.
- Use specific, descriptive headings rather than vague headings.
- Define important terms clearly.
- Add useful examples and use cases where they help the reader.
- Use bullets and tables when they make information easier to scan; do not use them decoratively.
- Keep the tone conversational and readable.
- Keep sentences and paragraphs readable for a Grade 8 target.
- Place callouts where they support the surrounding section.

## Required content structure

The draft must include:

- One H1.
- A direct answer of 40–70 words.
- Three to five key-takeaway bullets.
- A coherent heading hierarchy with no skipped levels.
- One to three callouts.
- Three to six FAQ questions, each answered in 40–80 words.
- A conclusion that follows the answer-first approach.

## Strategic requirements carried by the draft

- **Unique angle:** the piece must have a distinguishing angle rather than repeat generic knowledge.
- **Internal links:** relevant commercial links from the approved shortlist must appear naturally in the body, not only in the conclusion.
- **On-page elements:** draft the H1, meta title, meta description, Open Graph title and description, URL slug, image alt text, image filenames and FAQs with the body.

## Language and claims

- Use British English.
- Avoid absolute claims, banned phrasing and recognisable AI-tell phrasing.
- Avoid repeated adjectives beyond the configured threshold.
- Do not invent dimensions, materials, prices, delivery details, statistics, provenance or designer attribution.
- Provenance and designer-attribution statements always require operator review, even when a source appears to verify them.
